## Sales Reports and Analytics

Sales reports may be filtered by date, product, supplier, or category. Use a consistent date range and filters when comparing results, and verify that the report uses the intended business context.

Reports may summarize revenue, sales activity, or top-selling products. Use those findings to support decisions about product attention, stock, or marketing, while checking source records when a value is unexpected.

## Sales Forecasting

If a forecast view is available, it may use historical sales information to estimate future activity. Treat forecasts as planning input rather than a guarantee. Consider events, seasonality, stock availability, and changes to the product range when interpreting a projection.

## Subscribers and Marketing Lists

Subscriber tools may display users who have opted in to communications. Respect subscription preferences and applicable consent requirements. Use imports or audience lists only when the source and intended use are approved.

Before sending a campaign, confirm the audience, message, and unsubscribe or preference handling required by your organization.

## Email Campaigns

Where email campaign tools are enabled, a careful campaign workflow includes:

1. Define the intended audience and purpose.
2. Draft the content and verify links, offer terms, and dates.
3. Review the sender and audience settings.
4. Preview and test the message if the system provides those options.
5. Obtain required approval before scheduling or sending.
6. Review delivery and engagement reporting afterward.

Do not send messages to users who have not provided the required consent.

## Campaign Measurement

Campaign reports may show opens, clicks, or other engagement information. Interpret these metrics in context, using the same period and audience when comparing campaigns. Metrics can help identify patterns but do not prove that a campaign caused a sale.
