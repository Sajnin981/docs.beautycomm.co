A good support request makes the investigation faster and more accurate. The more clear and specific the details, the easier it is for the team to identify the issue and provide a helpful resolution.

## Include the essentials

Share the following when possible:

- the page or workflow affected
- the date and time the issue occurred
- the account role and business context involved
- the expected behavior versus the actual behavior
- any error messages or warning prompts
- the item, order, product, or customer involved, if relevant
- any screenshots or recordings that show the problem

## Helpful examples

Instead of saying, “The order page is broken,” try:

- “The order detail page fails to load when I filter by status on Thursday afternoon.”
- “A product record is not saving after I update pricing and inventory details.”
- “The customer search returns no results even though the record exists in the active business context.”

## Keep the report focused

Avoid sending long descriptions without context. Include the exact workflow, the issue symptoms, and any evidence that helps confirm the problem.

## Before you send the request

Double-check that you have:

- the correct issue summary
- relevant screenshots or timestamps
- the correct business or environment name
- any steps needed to reproduce the problem
